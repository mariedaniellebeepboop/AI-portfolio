Data operations & automation

AI-Assisted Customer Intelligence & Data Operations

A workflow that turns fragmented customer data into automated pipelines, autonomous agents, and metrics customers can actually act on, including a safety insight built by connecting data that used to sit apart.

The problem

Customer, product, support, device, and compliance data lived in disconnected systems with no centralized intelligence layer. Every question meant manual extraction and reconciliation, effectively making one person the organization's human data warehouse.

What I built

  • AI-assisted SQL pipelines against Snowflake, plus reconciliation and validation that caught incorrect metric logic before it shipped
  • Customer-health models and automated delivery of hard metrics into Totango and Domo, so other teams could self-serve
  • The first automation now runs fully headless, with about 10 to 15 more being converted from manual to autonomous, on the way to an orchestrator that runs and builds sub-agents as needed
  • A cross-product proof of concept linking safety alerts to duty-cycle hours, route, and GPS data to surface driver-fatigue patterns, and customers responded well when I showed them an early version
View sample deliverable: customer value-review deck (PDF)

Salesforce Case Feed held the most useful support detail but wasn't reportable through standard tools, so teams manually read case histories to spot recurring issues. That approach broke down at scale: large customers ran 200 to 600 cases, and all-customer review meant tens of thousands monthly.

I designed a workflow (via Gemini CLI, plain-English interaction) merging standard case fields with Case Feed data pulled through the API, then analyzing the consolidated set for trends, problem vehicles, and root-cause indicators.

  • Validated against issues Support leadership had already reviewed manually. The AI reached the same conclusions every time, and once found a root cause across hundreds of cases in under five minutes
  • Operational impact: one analysis flagged specific problem vehicles, leading directly to targeted equipment replacement
  • My role: problem framing, workflow and prompt design, directing script/API build-out, testing, leadership validation, and rollout to other teams, rather than manual engineering

Release & migration governance

Enterprise Release Readiness & Adoption Intelligence

A system that tracks who's eligible for a rollout, where they're stuck, and what's blocking them. Built for a 1,700-customer SaaS release and extended to a compliance-sensitive ELD migration with an autonomous risk-flagging agent.

Case study A: the 1,700-customer rollout

Six planned milestones didn't map cleanly to reality: some unlocked no customers because features weren't ready, while the ones that did unlock customers evolved into further sub-releases. The data needed to manage it was scattered across 8–10 systems that kept changing shape underneath the project.

  • Built an eligibility model across ~1,700 accounts with milestone and sub-release logic, adoption funnel, Jira onboarding/staleness tracking, and leadership dashboards
  • Caught a data-quality bug, duplicate join rows across snapshot dates, that was silently corrupting dashboard metrics
  • Separated customer-specific onboarding blockers from near-universal product gaps, routing the latter to Product instead of individual CSMs

The same eligibility/monitoring approach, applied to a higher-stakes migration: moving ELD customers off a legacy platform onto a new one, under an aggressive deadline and a two-hop migration path that had never been tested.

  • Built an autonomous, headless agent that pulls data via API to flag migration issues daily to leadership and engineering, catching problems before support has to escalate them
  • Compiled undocumented migration "gotchas" from people with prior experience, since none of it existed in writing
  • Pushed to formalize a risk log for risks nobody had named out loud: the untested two-hop path, a fully untracked cohort sitting under a hard deadline, undocumented onboarding issues, and the compliance exposure of a milestone-based rollout for a regulated product

This case study extends the first: not just monitoring a rollout, but building the system that catches problems before they're reported.
